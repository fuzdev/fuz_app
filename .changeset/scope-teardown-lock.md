fix: a scope's teardown and the writes into the scope take turns at a scope lock

- fix: `query_role_grant_revoke_for_scope` takes the scope's advisory lock exclusive as its first statement, and the scoped writes take it shared: `role_grant_offer_create` and `role_grant_assign` before the consumer's `authorize` callback runs, `query_role_grant_offer_create` and `query_create_role_grant` first thing, and `query_accept_offer` after its unlocked head read, before any row. Before, an offer or an assign authorized against a scope before or during its teardown could insert after the teardown committed, leaving a pending offer or a live grant at a dead scope. Now a callback that reads the scope reads it after any teardown that committed first and refuses the dead scope — the existing 403 `role_grant_offer_not_authorized` with its failure row — and a teardown that starts after a write waits for it, then revokes its grant or supersedes its offer. An accept of an offer at a torn-down scope waits and then answers `role_grant_offer_terminal`. Unscoped writes take nothing new. A consumer teardown must call `query_role_grant_revoke_for_scope` before it touches its scope row (or take `query_scope_lock_exclusive` first): one that locks the row first deadlocks with a callback that locks it, and one that deletes the row first leaves the window open. pglet needs `pg_advisory_xact_lock_shared`. Twin of the Rust spine
