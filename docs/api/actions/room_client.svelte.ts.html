actions/room_client.svelte.ts

The frontend side of rooms: RoomClient keeps, for each room a page joins or watches, a reactive RoomState — its members and their state, the caller's own seat — over the WS-only room_* verbs and the room_* notifications the server pushes (actions/room_action_specs.ts).

Desired state, converged one request at a time

RoomState.join, leave, watch, unwatch, and set_state record what the page wants; one runner per room then sends whatever requests close the gap, one at a time. The server dispatches a socket's requests concurrently, so a leave sent right after a join could otherwise reach the registry first (and refuse the join as superseded), and two state sets could land out of order. A burst of calls converges: join, leave, join while the first join is in flight sends that one join, whose seat stands; a leave while seated is sent before the next join. While the socket is down nothing is sent and the wants wait for it. RoomState.send is the exception: it goes out at once, since a caller that cares about the order of its sends (the WebRTC mesh, rtc/rtc_mesh.svelte.ts) serializes them itself.

Every verb runs untracked, so a page may call join or watch from an $effect (with leave or unwatch in its teardown) without the effect depending on the room state the verb reads and writes.

Listeners are isolated: one that throws is logged and the others still hear the event, and the join, replay, or teardown that called it carries on.

seq

Every member event carries its room's seq, and a join or watch answers the room as of a seq. Events can arrive before that answer, so while a room's first answer is outstanding they are buffered; the answer's members are applied, then the buffer replays, dropping every event at or below the answer's seq. Once a room is live, an event at or below the last applied seq is dropped, so a connection that both watches a room and sits in it, which the server tells each event once, applies it once. A later answer whose seq is behind the live stream is not applied over it. A message (which reaches only a seat) that arrives on a live watch while the join is in flight is held, and delivered once the seat is taken.

Seats lost, and reconnects

room_seat_lost ends a seat the server took: moved (a newer join of the same actor took over, moved_to naming its room), evicted, or room_closed. None is rejoined. A socket that closes loses every seat and watch with it, and the client tells them apart: a seat lost to the disconnect is rejoined on the next connection, naming the seat it held (rejoin_of), and a refusal as room_seat_moved — the actor took a seat elsewhere meanwhile — ends it as moved, so two tabs never trade a seat back and forth. A rejoin refused rate_limited is retried after the refusal's retry_after, still naming the seat, up to ROOM_REJOIN_RATE_LIMITED_RETRIES_MAX times. Watches are taken again.

The client learns of a new connection through RoomRpc.connection, an identity that changes each time the socket opens. The client follows it on its own: its constructor makes an $effect.root whose effect reads it and calls sync_connection, so a close and a reopen are seen even when they land in one batch, and dispose ends it. On the server the compiler makes $effect.root a no-op, and nothing follows; sync_connection stays public for that case and for tests, and runs on its own before each request and notification.

const rooms = new RoomClient(create_room_rpc(socket));

Notifications reach the client through receive, from the RPC client's lookup_action_handler for the 'receive' phase (with room_notification_action_specs among the RPC client's specs):

lookup_action_handler: (method, phase) => phase === 'receive' && is_room_notification_method(method) ? (event) => rooms.receive(method, event.data.input) : undefined

dispose leaves every seat and drops every watch, for a clean teardown.
